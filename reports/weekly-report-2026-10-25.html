Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Paid social is carrying the traffic load at 50% of all sessions this week, and while that influx has diluted the overall conversion rate to 2.19% against a 3.0% forecast, the sheer volume has still pushed online sales to $242,741 — up 80% on last year.
Average order value of $158 is tracking above both last year ($136) and forecast ($140), which means customers who do buy are spending more, helping offset the lower conversion rate.
Needs Attention
The BFF+ is moving 730 units a week but only has 2.2 weeks of stock cover, making it the most urgent inventory risk in the range right now.
Conversion rate is sitting 0.81 percentage points below forecast, and with paid social driving half of all sessions, it is worth checking whether landing page experience or offer relevance is letting those visitors down.
This week: Flag the BFF+ stock position to the buying team today and confirm whether a replenishment order can be expedited before cover drops below one week.
Paid Media
Working
Paid media is delivering a return on ad spend of 5.4x against a forecast of 3.0x, and a cost per order of $29 against a forecast of $65, meaning the channel is performing at roughly half the expected cost for nearly double the expected return.
Google spend has scaled from $2,190 to $8,293 versus last year, with the BFF and BFF+ dedicated Shopping campaigns and PMAX collectively driving the bulk of that incremental reach at an efficient cost.
Needs Attention
Meta spend is down from $42,443 to $36,364 year on year, so the overall efficiency gains are being driven largely by Google scaling up rather than Meta improving — it is worth understanding whether Meta is being deliberately pulled back or if delivery has softened.
Organic social is only contributing 2% of sessions (1,128), which is low given the brand's content presence, suggesting paid social is doing the heavy lifting that owned social should be supplementing.
This week: Pull the Meta campaign breakdown this week to confirm whether the lower spend versus last year is a deliberate budget shift or a delivery issue, and adjust accordingly before next week's budget is locked.
Retention & Revenue
Working
The Seeker launch email hit a 58.9% open rate and generated $1,341 in revenue, which is a solid result for a new product introduction and signals strong list engagement for product news.
New customer mix of 64.5% is almost exactly on the 65% forecast, which means acquisition and retention are in healthy balance this week.
Needs Attention
The VIP Pop-Up invite had a strong 57.1% open rate but generated zero direct revenue, so while list engagement is healthy, the email is not converting — it may need a clearer call to action or an incentive to drive attendance or a linked purchase.
Returns have nearly doubled in units (60 versus 32 last year) alongside the volume spike, and while the 2.8% return rate is only slightly above last year's 2.7%, it is worth monitoring product-level return reasons as BFF+ scales.
This week: Review the VIP Pop-Up invite click-through data in Klaviyo and test adding a direct purchase link or exclusive offer to the follow-up send to convert that open rate into revenue.
