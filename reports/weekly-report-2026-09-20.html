Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Paid social is carrying 46% of all sessions and, combined with a conversion rate of 2.99% — almost exactly on forecast — it is translating traffic into orders efficiently at scale.
Average order value of $151 is running $11 ahead of forecast, which is lifting online revenue even on weeks where session volume softens.
Needs Attention
The return rate has jumped to 3.4% (63 units, $8,443) compared to 2.2% last year, so it is worth investigating whether a particular product or size is driving the increase before it compounds over a bigger sales base.
Organic search is contributing only 7% of sessions and organic social just 1%, meaning almost all growth is being bought — building owned traffic would reduce reliance on paid spend to sustain these results.
This week: Pull the returns data by product and size this week to identify whether The BFF+ (the top seller with no prior year comparison) is driving the spike, and flag any patterns to the product team.
Paid Media
Working
Overall paid media is delivering a return on ad spend of 6.1x against a forecast of 3.0x, and a cost per order of $25 against a forecast of $65, meaning the business is getting more than double the expected efficiency from every dollar spent.
Meta is doing the heavy lifting at $27,656 spend and is clearly the engine behind the 46% paid social session share, with the volume of new customers (70.5%) suggesting prospecting creative is resonating well.
Needs Attention
The Demand Gen campaign for The Seeker spent only $222 this week, which is very low for a prospecting campaign on a product that moved 142 units — it may be worth testing a higher budget here while performance is strong.
Google spend has grown 183% year on year to $7,073, spread across multiple Shopping and Search campaigns, so it is worth consolidating campaign structure to avoid the PMAX and standard Shopping campaigns competing against each other for the same inventory.
This week: Review whether the PMAX campaign and the two standard Shopping prospecting campaigns are cannibalising each other, and consolidate or add audience signals to the PMAX to give Google's algorithm clearer direction.
Retention & Revenue
Working
Both email campaigns this week delivered strong open rates above 53%, suggesting the subject lines and send-time logic are well calibrated for the current list.
Average order value is $150 across the brand, up from $125 last year, which points to customers either buying multiple items or trading up — the outfitting angle in the Main Squeeze campaign may be contributing to this.
Needs Attention
Email drove only 5% of sessions (2,204), which is modest given the open rates achieved — there is likely an opportunity to send more frequently or introduce an automated flow to convert that engaged audience into more revenue.
New customers make up 70.5% of orders this week, well above the 65% forecast, which is positive for growth but means retention campaigns need to work harder over the coming weeks to convert first-time buyers into repeat purchasers.
This week: Set up or review the post-purchase email sequence for new customers acquired this week, ensuring there is a follow-up touchpoint within seven days that highlights a complementary product to the one they bought.
