Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Paid social is carrying the traffic load at 43% of all sessions and is clearly the engine behind the week's strong volume growth, while direct traffic at 12% suggests some solid brand recall is building alongside it.
Conversion rate held at 2.37% against more than double last year's session volume, which means the site is handling scale well even if it's still sitting below the 3.0% forecast.
Needs Attention
Organic search and organic social together account for only 7% of sessions, which is a thin foundation if paid spend ever pulls back — both channels are underperforming relative to the overall mix.
The Sneaky Jane is the one product moving backwards, down from 41 to 31 units sold week on week against peers that are all growing, worth understanding whether this is a ranging or visibility issue.
This week: Run a quick audit on organic search and organic social this week to identify whether low session share is a content gap, an SEO issue, or simply crowded out by paid — and flag one concrete fix for each.
Paid Media
Working
Overall paid media delivered a return on ad spend of 5.0x against a forecast of 3.0x, with a cost per order of $31 versus a forecast of $65, meaning every dollar spent is working roughly twice as hard as planned.
Meta at $21,902 spend is driving the lion's share of paid volume and is clearly the primary growth lever this week, with paid social sessions more than doubling year on year.
Needs Attention
Google's Demand Gen campaign for The Seeker spent only $162 for the week, which is too small a budget to read performance meaningfully for what is the brand's fourth-biggest product by units.
Shopping campaigns across three separate line items totalled $3,390 in Google spend with overlapping naming conventions — it is worth confirming these are not cannibalising each other or splitting budget inefficiently.
This week: Consolidate or clearly differentiate the three Google Shopping campaigns this week to ensure budget is being allocated intentionally rather than split across overlapping product sets.
Retention & Revenue
Working
The Sophie Pearce styled content email on 27 August was the standout performer, generating $5,658 in revenue with a 54.2% open rate, showing that creator or editorial framing resonates strongly with the list.
Average order value online came in at $153 against a forecast of $140 and well above last year's $133, suggesting returning and new customers alike are adding more to their carts — likely helped by the BFF+ launch.
Needs Attention
The Lullaby Club Preview email generated only $963 in revenue despite a healthy 49.8% open rate, which points to strong curiosity but a landing experience or offer that is not converting browsers into buyers.
New customers made up 72.6% of orders against a forecast of 65%, which is a great acquisition week but means fewer returning customers than expected are activating — worth watching whether the new cohort comes back.
This week: Review the Lullaby Club Preview landing page or linked product this week to identify why a nearly 50% open rate did not convert to more sales, and test one change before the next send.
