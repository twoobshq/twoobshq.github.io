Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Online sales are up 67% on last year, driven by a healthy mix of more sessions and a stronger conversion rate (2.69% vs 2.22% last year), with average order value also ahead of forecast at $153.
Paid social is doing the heavy lifting at 43% of all sessions, while direct and organic search are each contributing a steady 12%, suggesting solid brand awareness alongside the paid push.
Needs Attention
Conversion rate at 2.69% is still sitting below the 3.0% forecast, meaning there are incremental orders being left on the table despite strong traffic volume.
Returns have jumped to 3.9% of sales value versus 2.2% last year — worth monitoring whether this is driven by a specific product or size issue before it becomes a bigger drag on net revenue.
This week: Investigate the return reasons for the 31 units returned this week to identify whether a specific style, size, or customer segment is driving the uptick, and flag for the product team if a pattern emerges.
Paid Media
Working
Overall paid media is performing well above forecast — return on ad spend came in at 4.8x against a forecast of 3.0x, and cost per order was just $32 against a forecast of $65, meaning the team is getting roughly twice the efficiency expected.
Meta is carrying the bulk of spend at $15,394 and appears to be the primary engine behind the 43% paid social session share, which is converting efficiently given the strong overall paid cost per order.
Needs Attention
Google spend is split across three campaigns with the unbranded shopping and Performance Max campaigns each spending over $1,300, but with no campaign-level return on ad spend visible it is worth confirming which of the three is actually delivering orders rather than just traffic.
Paid search is only generating 2% of sessions despite being a separate budget line, suggesting it may be underfunded or underperforming relative to the shopping and Performance Max activity.
This week: Pull campaign-level cost per order for all three Google campaigns this week to confirm whether the Performance Max or unbranded shopping campaign is the more efficient driver, and reallocate budget accordingly.
Retention & Revenue
Working
Both BFF+ relaunch emails this week achieved strong open rates — 49.3% and 56.2% respectively — confirming that the BFF+ restock was a genuinely anticipated moment for the customer base.
Average order value online is running at $153 against a forecast of $140, and with 63.2% new customers (close to the 65% forecast), the business is growing its base without sacrificing spend per order.
Needs Attention
Combined email revenue of just under $2,000 this week is relatively modest given the list size implied by those open rates, suggesting the BFF+ relaunch emails may have been sent to a smaller segment or that click-through to purchase dropped off after the open.
Pom Pom Thingys are moving fast at 59 units with only 11.8 weeks of stock cover remaining — if this product has a strong returning customer base, a targeted retention email before it sells out could capture demand before stock becomes a constraint.
This week: Schedule a targeted email to customers who have previously purchased accessories or the original BFF to promote Pom Pom Thingys while stock cover is still above 10 weeks.
