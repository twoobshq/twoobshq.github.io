Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Overall sales are nearly double last year at $210,085 online, driven by a 121% surge in sessions — paid social alone accounts for 52% of all traffic and is clearly the engine behind the volume lift.
Average order value of $160 is tracking above both last year ($137) and forecast ($140), with new customers at 67.6% — just ahead of the 65% forecast — suggesting the brand is successfully reaching and converting fresh audiences.
Needs Attention
Conversion rate of 2.35% is meaningfully below last year's 3.13% and the 3.0% forecast, which makes sense given the flood of cold paid social traffic, but it's worth watching whether landing page experience or offer strength needs sharpening for that audience.
Organic social (620 sessions, 1%) and email (3,091 sessions, 6%) are both punching well below their weight relative to the overall session mix — email in particular has room to contribute more given the size of the list.
This week: Run an A/B test on the paid social landing page this week — try a version that leads with a customer review or a clearer size-guide prompt to see if it lifts the conversion rate closer to the 3.0% forecast.
Paid Media
Working
Return on ad spend of 4.6x is well ahead of both last year (4.0x) and the 3.0x forecast, and cost per order of $35 is dramatically below the $65 forecast, meaning the team is getting far more efficient results from a higher spend base — a strong sign the creative and targeting are landing.
Meta is carrying the bulk of the load at $37,999 spend and is clearly driving the paid social session surge, while Google's three Shopping campaigns are collectively spending $5,242 and providing solid lower-funnel support without cannibalising the Meta efficiency.
Needs Attention
The PMAX prospecting campaign ($2,383) and the two broad Shopping prospecting campaigns ($1,945 and $1,314) have overlapping audience intent — it's worth checking whether they are competing against each other in auction and inflating costs unnecessarily.
Google spend has jumped from $2,314 to $8,161 year on year, so it's a good week to verify that the incremental volume from Search and Shopping is genuinely new demand and not just capturing clicks that organic or direct would have delivered anyway.
This week: Pull the auction insights report for the three Google Shopping and PMAX campaigns to check for overlap, and pause or tighten the lower-performing one if they are bidding against each other.
Retention & Revenue
Working
The BFF+ in black email sent on 12 October achieved a 49.5% open rate, which is an excellent result and shows the list is engaged with new product launches — $1,747 in direct revenue from a single campaign is a solid return.
Return rate has dropped to 2.0% from 3.5% last year on higher volume, suggesting customers are happy with what they are receiving — this reduces friction in the post-purchase experience and supports repeat purchase intent.
Needs Attention
Only one email campaign was sent this week, and email drove just 6% of sessions (3,091) despite the brand having a much larger customer base than last year — there is a clear opportunity to send more to the returning customer segment, which at 32.4% of buyers is a meaningful group.
The BFF+ generated 698 units sold with only 4.3 weeks of stock cover, so retention communications promoting this product need to be carefully timed to avoid driving demand the brand cannot fulfil.
This week: Schedule a targeted win-back or cross-sell email this week aimed at customers who have previously bought The BFF or The OG, introducing them to The BFF+ before stock cover tightens further.
