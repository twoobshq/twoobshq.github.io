Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Online sales are up 79% on last year driven by a 133% surge in sessions, with paid social alone accounting for 55% of all traffic — the volume is clearly there and converting profitably.
Average order value has lifted to $156 against a forecast of $140, and the return rate has improved to 2.9% from 3.7% last year, meaning more revenue is sticking.
Needs Attention
Conversion rate at 1.97% is well below the 3.0% forecast and last year's 2.86%, which suggests the flood of new paid social traffic is lower intent and dragging the overall rate down.
Organic social is a very small slice of the session mix at just 2% (1,312 sessions), which is worth watching given how heavily the site is leaning on paid social to carry the load.
This week: Run a quick audit of the landing pages receiving paid social traffic to identify any friction points — load speed, offer clarity, or size guidance — that could be suppressing conversion rate this week.
Paid Media
Working
Paid media is delivering a return on ad spend of 5.6x against a forecast of 3.0x, with a cost per order of $28 versus a forecast of $65 — both Meta and Google are punching well above expectations this week.
Google Shopping campaigns for All Products and BFF/BFF+ together account for nearly half of Google spend and are driving efficient prospecting volume at a time when the brand has strong stock cover across its top styles.
Needs Attention
Total paid spend is $45,297 — down 23% on last year's $58,930 — so while efficiency is strong, there may be room to increase budget and capture more demand while return on ad spend remains this healthy.
The PMAX prospecting campaign at $2,166 is the largest single Google line item but its performance relative to the Shopping campaigns is worth scrutinising, as PMAX can quietly absorb budget from better-performing placements.
This week: Test a modest Meta budget increase of 15 to 20% this week while return on ad spend is at 5.6x, prioritising creative that highlights The Seeker and Rosy Thingys given their limited stock and urgency potential.
Retention & Revenue
Working
The Sticky Fig Launch Early Access email on 13 November was a standout, generating $16,950 in revenue with a 48.3% open rate — early access framing is clearly resonating with the subscriber base.
Average order value online is $156 against a forecast of $140, and returning customers are spending confidently, suggesting loyalty and product satisfaction are in good shape heading into peak season.
Needs Attention
The Pop-Up Recap email on 15 November pulled only $2,043 in revenue despite a solid 44.3% open rate, which points to a disconnect between the content and purchase intent rather than a deliverability or audience issue.
New customers make up 62.4% of orders this week — close to the 65% forecast — but with Rosy Thingys sitting at just 3.6 weeks of stock cover, there is a risk that new buyers drawn in this week cannot be retained into a second purchase if key styles sell out.
This week: Build a post-purchase flow specifically for this week's new customers that highlights complementary styles and Protecty Stuff add-ons, so the strong acquisition volume this week converts into repeat buyers before peak season ends.
