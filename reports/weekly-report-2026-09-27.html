Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Paid Social is doing the heavy lifting this week, accounting for 47% of all sessions and helping drive a 231% lift in total traffic versus last year, which is the engine behind the strong overall sales result.
Average order value of $161 is running well above forecast ($140) and last year ($123), suggesting customers are buying more per visit — likely helped by the BFF+ launch and the new Bridal Collection driving higher-intent purchases.
Needs Attention
Conversion rate of 2.40% is sitting below both last year (3.42%) and forecast (3.0%), meaning the site is bringing in a lot more visitors but not converting them at the rate it should — worth checking whether landing page experience or product availability is creating friction for new visitors.
Rosy Thingys has only 0.5 weeks of stock cover left with 61 units sold last week, so a stockout is effectively imminent and could leave demand unmet if a reorder or waitlist isn't in place already.
This week: Audit the top paid social landing pages this week to identify any friction points — slow load times, weak product imagery, or unclear sizing information — that could be suppressing the conversion rate for the high volume of new traffic coming in.
Paid Media
Working
Overall paid media is delivering a return on ad spend of 5.5x against a forecast of 3.0x and a cost per order of $29 versus a $65 forecast, so every dollar spent is working significantly harder than planned across both Meta and Google.
Meta is driving the bulk of paid volume at $34,601 spend and is clearly the primary engine behind the 28,396 paid social sessions — the scale and efficiency together suggest current creative and audience targeting are well matched to the offer.
Needs Attention
The Demand Gen campaign for The Seeker spent only $247 this week, which is a very small allocation for a hiker rebrand that has already sold 114 units and has a healthy 10.8 weeks of stock cover — there may be room to scale this campaign more aggressively while inventory supports it.
Google is running multiple overlapping Shopping campaigns (All Products, a second prospecting campaign, and a Brand Shopping campaign) which risks budget fragmentation and keyword cannibalisation — worth reviewing whether consolidation into fewer, cleaner campaigns would improve efficiency.
This week: Increase the Demand Gen budget for The Seeker campaign this week and test a second ad set targeting outdoor and hiking interest audiences, while the product has strong stock cover and clear sales momentum.
Retention & Revenue
Working
The Bridal Collection early access email generated $10,551 in revenue with a 46.8% open rate, showing that giving a segment of customers first access to a new collection is a strong mechanic for driving immediate purchases.
New customers represent 66.1% of orders this week, landing right on forecast and above last year's 52.6%, which reflects the paid media investment is successfully reaching and converting new audiences rather than just reactivating existing ones.
Needs Attention
The public Bridal Collection launch email generated only $1,083 despite a higher open rate of 59.1%, suggesting the general audience was curious but far less likely to buy — this gap is worth investigating to understand whether price point, styling, or relevance is the barrier for the broader list.
With 66% of orders coming from new customers this week, the returning customer base is relatively quiet — this is a good week to run a targeted retention send to lapsed buyers who haven't purchased in 60 to 90 days, while brand momentum is high.
This week: Pull a segment of customers who purchased The BFF or The OG in the last six months but have not bought again, and send them a targeted email this week introducing the BFF+ as the natural next step — linking the new product to something they already love.
