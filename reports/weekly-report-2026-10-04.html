Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Overall online sales are up 164% on last year, driven by a surge in new customers (68.6% of orders) that well exceeds the 65% forecast, suggesting the current acquisition push is landing with a genuinely fresh audience.
Store sales nearly tripled last year's result at $21,758, with a healthy average order value of $145, showing the physical retail experience is pulling its weight alongside the online channel.
Needs Attention
Conversion rate at 2.61% is sitting below both last year (3.12%) and the 3.0% forecast, meaning the spike in paid social traffic is bringing in browsers who aren't quite converting at the rate we'd expect — worth investigating whether landing pages or product pages are doing enough work.
Returns have jumped sharply to 5.4% of sales (109 units, $13,860) versus 3.2% last year, and with The BFF+ accounting for 800 units sold in its first week, it's worth monitoring whether sizing or expectation gaps are driving this early return behaviour.
This week: Pull the returns data by product this week to confirm whether The BFF+ is the primary driver of the elevated return rate, and if so, review the product page sizing guide and imagery before another wave of orders lands.
Paid Media
Working
Total paid media is delivering a return on ad spend of 4.5x against a 3.0x forecast and a cost per order of $32 against a $65 forecast — both comfortably ahead of target, which means the current spend level of $45,727 is working efficiently across both platforms.
Meta at $38,475 is clearly the primary driver of volume, accounting for the bulk of the 52% paid social session share, and the efficiency metrics suggest the creative and targeting mix is in a strong place right now.
Needs Attention
Google's prospecting campaigns (PMAX and two Shopping lines) are collectively spending $5,123 this week, but with paid search and paid shopping together making up only 16% of sessions, it's worth confirming these campaigns are generating orders at a cost per order that justifies the split versus Meta.
Organic social delivered just 597 sessions (1% of total) against paid social's 27,920, which is a very wide gap — while paid is performing well, this level of imbalance means the brand is heavily reliant on spend to drive social traffic and has little organic buffer if costs rise.
This week: Pull cost per order by individual Google campaign this week and pause or reallocate budget from any prospecting line where cost per order is above $65, redirecting that spend to Meta where efficiency is strongest.
Retention & Revenue
Working
The OOO final email sent 30 August generated $13,858 in revenue with a 52.4% open rate, showing the audience is highly engaged with product comeback messaging and that a well-timed urgency send can still be a meaningful revenue driver.
Average order value online at $144 is tracking above the $140 forecast and well above last year's $123, suggesting customers are either buying more per order or trading into higher-priced styles like The BFF+.
Needs Attention
The OOO pre-order email on 2 October had the highest open rate of the two sends at 74.8% but generated less than half the revenue ($6,584), which suggests strong interest but hesitation to commit to a pre-order — the email conversion from open to purchase needs attention.
Email drove only 2,529 sessions this week (5% of total), which is a small share for a channel that should be a low-cost, high-converting workhorse, especially given the size of the new customer cohort coming in that will need nurturing into a second purchase.
This week: This week, build and schedule a post-purchase flow specifically for new BFF+ buyers — a short sequence that sets delivery expectations, addresses sizing questions, and plants the seed for a second purchase to start converting the large new customer influx into repeat buyers.
