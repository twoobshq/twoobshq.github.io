Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Paid social is driving the session surge, accounting for 56% of all traffic and pushing total sessions up 66% on last year, which is the primary reason new customers are tracking above forecast at 66.5%.
Store sales are the standout this week, up over 1,100% on last year to $114,427, lifting brand-level average order value to $176 and pulling total brand net sales to $301,349.
Needs Attention
Online conversion rate has dropped to 1.77% against a forecast of 3.0% and last year's 2.70%, meaning the volume of paid social traffic is not converting efficiently enough to fully capitalise on the session growth.
Email is contributing just 4% of sessions despite the brand having an active subscriber base, which suggests the channel is underleveraged relative to the paid traffic being bought this week.
This week: Run a conversion audit on the paid social landing pages this week — check load speed, product page layout, and add-to-cart friction for first-time visitors to close the gap between traffic volume and conversion rate.
Paid Media
Working
Paid media is delivering strong efficiency, with return on ad spend at 4.1x against a forecast of 3.0x and cost per order at $36 versus a forecast of $65, achieved on 25% less spend than last year.
The two BFF+ and BFF-focused Shopping campaigns combined are spending $3,911 and aligning well with the top two selling products this week, supporting both volume and relevance.
Needs Attention
Meta spend has been cut significantly from $57,892 to $36,003 year on year, and while efficiency looks good now, the heavy reliance on Meta for 56% of all site sessions means any fatigue or audience saturation will have an outsized impact on overall traffic.
Google's Performance Max campaign at $2,615 is the largest single Google spend but its contribution is not broken out separately, making it difficult to tell whether it is the Shopping or Max activity driving Google's results.
This week: Pull the placement and asset group breakdown for the Performance Max campaign this week to confirm where spend is going and whether it is cannibalising the Shopping campaigns rather than adding incremental reach.
Retention & Revenue
Working
The Pop-Up Exclusive Colour preview email sent on 4 November generated $17,930 in revenue from a single send with a 53.2% open rate, showing that exclusivity-framed campaigns land well with this list.
Average order value online has lifted to $150 against a forecast of $140, and the return rate has improved to 3.1% from 3.5% last year, both pointing to customers buying more confidently and keeping what they purchase.
Needs Attention
Only one email campaign ran this week, which limits the retention channel's contribution to 4% of sessions at a time when paid media is working hard and an additional owned-channel touchpoint could convert warm visitors at near-zero marginal cost.
New customers now make up 66.5% of online buyers, which is healthy for growth but means the returning customer base needs active re-engagement soon to protect long-term repeat purchase rates.
This week: Schedule a returning-customer win-back or loyalty email for this week targeting buyers from the previous 90 days who have not yet purchased the BFF+ or The Seeker, using the strong new arrival narrative as the hook.
