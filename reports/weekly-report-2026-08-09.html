Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Online sales more than doubled versus last year to $96,317, driven primarily by paid social which brought in nearly half of all sessions and a conversion rate of 2.74% — well above last year's 1.65%.
Average order value of $155 is running above both last year and forecast, suggesting customers are buying more confidently, likely supported by the BFF+ launch attracting a strong new customer mix at 70.5%.
Needs Attention
Returns have jumped sharply to $6,898 and 46 units this week versus just $565 and 5 units last year, a return rate of 5.9% that warrants investigation into which styles or sizes are coming back.
Email is only delivering 3% of sessions and generated no revenue from either campaign sent this week, which is underperforming relative to the size of the customer base given how well other channels are converting.
This week: Pull the returns data by product and size this week to identify whether the spike is concentrated in the BFF+ launch and flag any fit or quality issues before volume grows further.
Paid Media
Working
Paid media is delivering a return on ad spend of 4.2x against a forecast of 3.0x, with a cost per order of $37 versus a forecast of $65, meaning the team is acquiring customers at nearly half the expected cost.
Meta at $19,064 spend is clearly the engine driving the bulk of paid performance, accounting for the lion's share of the 11,032 paid social sessions that made up 49% of all traffic this week.
Needs Attention
Google Ads spend is split across three campaigns — PMAX, and two overlapping Shopping campaigns — and with only $3,767 total spend it is worth confirming these are not cannibalising each other or duplicating coverage on the same products.
Paid Search brought in just 51 sessions this week despite being a separate line item, suggesting branded or non-branded search capture via this campaign type is almost negligible and may not be worth the budget allocation.
This week: Review the two Google Shopping campaigns this week to confirm they are targeting distinct product sets or audiences and consolidate if there is meaningful overlap.
Retention & Revenue
Working
Average order value of $155 is above both last year's $141 and the $140 forecast, suggesting returning customers in particular are spending more per visit, likely supported by accessories like Protecty Stuff and Pom Pom Thingys attaching to footwear orders.
The BFF+ launched with 300 units sold this week with no prior year comparable, indicating strong new product adoption that will seed a fresh cohort of customers to retain and re-engage over coming months.
Needs Attention
Both email campaigns sent this week were Brisbane VIP event invites that generated zero revenue, and while open rates of 42% to 57% are healthy, the list is not being used to drive online sales at a time when the store is converting well.
New customers make up 70.5% of orders this week against a forecast of 65%, which is a positive acquisition signal but means the returning customer base is contributing less than planned — worth monitoring as the BFF+ hype settles.
This week: Schedule a post-purchase email flow for BFF+ buyers this week to introduce complementary accessories and encourage a second purchase while brand engagement is high.
