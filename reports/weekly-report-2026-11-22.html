Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Online sales are up 68% on last year driven by a near-tripling of sessions, with paid social alone accounting for 58% of all traffic and clearly doing the heavy lifting this week.
Average order value of $160 is running above both last year ($142) and forecast ($140), suggesting customers are buying more per visit even as a large wave of new shoppers arrives.
Needs Attention
Conversion rate of 1.81% is well below last year's 3.57% and the 3.0% forecast, which is worth watching — the flood of paid social traffic is likely bringing in a colder, less purchase-ready audience that isn't converting at the same rate as the existing customer base.
Email drove only 4% of sessions this week, which is low relative to what a retained audience should contribute heading into the peak Black Friday period when owned channels typically punch harder.
This week: Run a quick audit of the paid social landing page experience this week to identify any friction points — slow load times, mismatched offers, or unclear calls to action — that may be suppressing conversion rate among the high volume of new visitors.
Paid Media
Working
Paid media is delivering a return on ad spend of 5.9x against a forecast of 3.0x, and a cost per order of $27 versus a forecast of $65, meaning every dollar spent is going much further than planned.
Google spend has been scaled up significantly — nearly tripling versus last year to $6,221 — with the BFF and BFF+ shopping campaign and branded search both contributing efficiently to a channel that tends to capture high-intent buyers.
Needs Attention
Meta spend was actually pulled back versus last year ($39,030 vs $41,032) even as it is driving the vast majority of sessions, so it is worth confirming whether that budget constraint is deliberate or whether there is room to push harder given the strong return on ad spend.
The Performance Max prospecting campaign ($892) and bottom-of-funnel search campaign ($406) are both running at relatively low spend, and without visibility into their individual returns it is hard to know if they are earning their place or quietly underperforming.
This week: Pull the cost per order and return on ad spend for the Performance Max and bottom-of-funnel search campaigns individually this week to decide whether to scale, pause, or reallocate that budget to the shopping campaigns that are more transparently working.
Retention & Revenue
Working
New customer mix of 65.8% is almost exactly on forecast and well up on last year's 48.4%, suggesting the top-of-funnel investment is successfully recruiting new buyers without cannibalising the existing base.
The return rate dropped to 1.9% from 2.9% last year, which means the higher volume of orders is not bringing a higher proportion of refunds — a good sign for product-fit and customer satisfaction.
Needs Attention
Both email campaigns this week — the Silken Tofu and new website launch sends — generated under $800 in revenue each, which is modest given the subscriber base and the timing just before Black Friday when email should be warming the audience hard.
With 65.8% of orders coming from new customers this week, there is a large cohort that has never bought before and will need a strong post-purchase sequence to convert them into repeat buyers — the window to make a good first impression is right now.
This week: Schedule and brief a dedicated Black Friday winback or early-access email to lapsed customers this week, separate from the new customer acquisition sends, to ensure the retained base is activated before the peak weekend.
