Analyst Notes
Ecommerce & Conversion
Working
Online sales are up 183% on last year, driven by a healthy mix of strong paid social traffic and a conversion rate of 2.80% that is well above last year's 1.67%, even if it sits just below the 3.0% forecast.
Paid social is carrying 42% of all sessions this week, which is doing its job, but organic search (9%), email (8%), and organic social (1%) are all punching below their weight relative to the paid load being carried.
Needs Attention
Organic social at just 1% of sessions and 328 visits suggests owned social content is not converting to clicks, which creates a dependency risk if paid spend is ever pulled back.
Pom Pom Thingys have only 19.2 weeks of stock cover at current velocity, and with no last-year comparison this appears to be a new line gaining traction faster than expected — worth watching closely.
This week: Check whether the Seeker and BFF+ product pages are optimised for organic search terms, given both are high-velocity new lines with zero last-year baseline and a combined 492 units sold this week.
Paid Media
Working
Meta is delivering a return on ad spend of 5.6x against a forecast of 3.0x, and a cost per order of $27 against a forecast of $65, meaning the brand is getting more than twice the efficiency it planned for this week.
Paid social and paid search channels combined account for roughly 56% of all sessions, and the cost efficiency suggests the current creative or audience targeting is resonating strongly right now.
Needs Attention
Google Ads shows as not applicable this week with no spend recorded, which means all $18,596 in paid spend is sitting with Meta alone — that concentration is worth monitoring in case Meta performance softens.
Spend is up 57% on last year to $18,596, so while returns are strong now, it is worth confirming whether the volume is being driven by one hero campaign or broadly spread, so there is no single point of failure.
This week: Pull the Meta campaign breakdown to identify which specific campaign or creative is responsible for the outsized return on ad spend this week, and flag it for budget protection heading into next week.
Retention & Revenue
Working
New customer acquisition is running at 66.4%, just ahead of the 65% forecast and well above last year's 47.7%, which suggests paid and owned channels are successfully reaching new audiences rather than just recycling existing buyers.
Average order value of $152 is above both last year ($128) and the $140 forecast, which points to customers buying more premium products or multiple items — likely helped by the BFF+ and Seeker gaining traction.
Needs Attention
The Seeker Relaunch email sent on 16 August generated no revenue and an 8% open rate, which is well below a healthy benchmark and suggests the subject line, send time, or audience segment needs revisiting before the next send.
The Brisbane VIP resend had a strong 66.7% open rate but also generated no revenue, which may indicate the offer or call to action inside the email is not compelling enough to drive a purchase from an otherwise engaged audience.
This week: Rewrite the Seeker Relaunch email with a stronger subject line and a clearer single call to action, then resend to the unopened segment early this week while the relaunch momentum is still fresh.
